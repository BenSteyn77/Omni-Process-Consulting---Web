How To Install Google Analytics On A Small Business Website

If your website cannot tell you where leads came from, it cannot tell you what marketing is worth paying for.

Analytics8 min readUpdated September 2026

The short version: The fastest path for most small businesses is to create a Google Analytics 4 property, install it through Site Kit if the site uses WordPress, or place the Google tag or Google Tag Manager code on every page if the site is static HTML. Then test that visits, form submissions, phone clicks, and ad conversions are being recorded before spending money on traffic.

Why Analytics Comes Before More Traffic

A lot of small business websites look finished from the outside but are blind behind the scenes. They may have a phone number, a contact form, service pages, and even paid ads running, but no clean answer to the basic questions that matter: where did the visitor come from, what did they do, and did they become a lead?

That is why analytics is not a technical extra. It is the instrument panel for your marketing. Without it, Google Ads, Facebook ads, local SEO, and retargeting all become guesswork.

For local service businesses, this matters even more because a lead may arrive through a phone call, a form fill, a click-to-call button, a Google Business Profile visit, or an after-hours enquiry. If those actions are not tracked, the business owner ends up judging marketing by gut feel instead of evidence.

What You Need Before You Start

Before installing anything, gather access to the website, Google account, domain, and any ad accounts. You do not need an expensive stack to begin, but you do need control over the places where tracking code is added.

  • A Google account for the business
  • Access to Google Analytics
  • Admin access to WordPress, the website builder, or the static HTML files
  • Access to Google Search Console if available
  • A list of the actions that count as leads: form submit, phone click, booking request, quote request, or payment

Do not skip the last item. Tracking pageviews is useful, but tracking lead actions is what connects marketing to revenue.

Option 1: Install Google Analytics With Site Kit On WordPress

For WordPress websites, Site Kit by Google is usually the simplest setup route. Google's Analytics help explains that Site Kit can connect Analytics to a WordPress site without needing to manually edit code.

The practical process is simple: install Site Kit, connect the business Google account, choose or create the GA4 property, allow Site Kit to place the tag, then confirm that visits are showing in Analytics real-time reporting.

This is the best route when the owner manages their own WordPress site and does not want to touch theme files. It is also easier to maintain during theme updates because the tracking connection lives through the plugin.

Option 2: Install The Google Tag On A Static HTML Site

For a simple HTML website, the Google tag can be added directly to the head section of every page. Google's tag documentation says the tag should be installed in the code of each page you want to measure.

On a static website like the OPC site, that means placing the same Google tag snippet before the closing head tag on the homepage, service pages, blog pages, and offer pages.

This is clean and fast when you only need Analytics and perhaps Google Ads conversion tracking. The weakness is that future changes require file edits unless the site is built with reusable templates.

Option 3: Use Google Tag Manager When You Need Flexibility

Google Tag Manager becomes useful when the site will run several tags: Google Analytics, Google Ads conversions, Meta Pixel, call tracking, form tracking, remarketing, and future events.

GTM is not automatically better for every small business. It is better when the business expects regular tracking changes and does not want the website code edited every time.

For a business entering paid ads, this is usually the more scalable route. It lets you add events like form_submit, phone_click, booking_click, and lead without rebuilding the site.

What To Track First

Start with the actions that prove commercial intent. A small business owner does not need fifty dashboard widgets on day one. They need a reliable view of leads.

  • Contact form submissions
  • Click-to-call button taps
  • Email clicks
  • Quote request button clicks
  • Booking or consultation clicks
  • Stripe checkout completion if payment is part of the funnel

Once those events are working, the marketing conversation changes. Instead of asking whether ads feel expensive, you can ask which campaigns, pages, and keywords are creating real opportunities.

Where This Connects To The Bigger Lead System

Analytics is only one layer. The full system also needs lead capture, fast follow-up, and attribution. A contact form that records the source but sits unanswered for two days is still a broken system.

That is why tracking should connect with CRM and marketing automation, paid ads management, and, for call-heavy service businesses, an AI receptionist or missed-call follow-up workflow.

Quick Answers

What is the easiest way to add Google Analytics to WordPress?

For most WordPress sites, Site Kit by Google is the easiest route because it connects Google Analytics without manually editing theme code.

Where does the Google Analytics tag go on an HTML website?

The Google tag normally goes in the head section of every page you want to measure.

Should a small business use Google Tag Manager?

Use Google Tag Manager when you expect to manage multiple tags or events, such as Google Ads conversions, Meta Pixel, phone clicks, and form submissions.

What should I track before running ads?

Track form submissions, phone clicks, booking clicks, checkout completions, and any action that represents a real lead or sale.
